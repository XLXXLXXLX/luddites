The Luddite rebellions of early nineteenth century England have since given their name to those [opposed to technology](https://daily.jstor.org/why-luddites-are-fashionable-again/). It’s debatable if the Luddites were ever _luddites_: they weren’t opposed to new technology per se, they were opposed to the devaluation of their own labor and the attack on their livelihoods by the introduction of new machines. They were machine-breakers because the machines were the easiest targets in what historian Eric Hobsbawm characterized as “[collective bargaining by riot](https://libcom.org/article/machine-breakers-eric-hobsbawm).”  
十九世纪初英国的卢德运动从此成为[反对科技](https://daily.jstor.org/why-luddites-are-fashionable-again/)人士的代名词。但卢德分子是否真的算是_卢德分子_ ，这一点尚存争议：他们并非反对新技术本身，而是反对新机器的引入导致自身劳动贬值，并威胁到他们的生计。他们之所以被称为“机器破坏者”，是因为机器是历史学家埃里克·霍布斯鲍姆所描述的“ [暴乱式集体谈判](https://libcom.org/article/machine-breakers-eric-hobsbawm) ”中最容易攻击的目标。

[![“Collaborate”](https://daily.jstor.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/jstor_collaborators_ad_in_text.jpg)![“Collaborate”](https://daily.jstor.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/jstor_collaborators_ad_mobile.jpg)](https://about.jstor.org/collaborate-with-jstor/)

Taking their name from the apocryphal Ned Ludd, an apprentice who supposedly smashed stocking-making frames a generation or two earlier, these rebellious textile workers and their allies were most active in northern England between 1811 and 1813. These were met with the brute force of English law, but only after initial trials were judged “[insufficient to instill enough terror to quell the rebellion](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.14321/jstudradi.14.1.0121?mag=luddites-on-trial),” writes historian Geoffrey Poitras.  
这些反叛的纺织工人及其盟友以虚构人物内德·卢德的名字命名，据说内德·卢德是一位学徒，在一两代人之前砸毁了织袜机。他们在 1811 年至 1813 年间在英格兰北部最为活跃。历史学家杰弗里·波伊特拉斯写道，他们遭到了英国法律的残酷镇压，但此前最初的审判被认为“ [不足以造成足够的威慑来平息叛乱](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.14321/jstudradi.14.1.0121?mag=luddites-on-trial) ”。

“No charges were made at the Luddite trials for capital offenses directly relevant to the revolutionary acts of treason and sedition,” he notes. “Rather, the trials involved many standard felony charges that risked capital penalties, such as burglary, robbery, arson, and ‘endeavouring by threat’ to obstruct justice.”  
他指出：“在卢德分子审判中，没有对与叛国和煽动叛乱等革命行为直接相关的死刑罪行提出指控。相反，审判涉及许多可能被判处死刑的普通重罪指控，例如入室盗窃、抢劫、纵火以及‘以威胁手段’妨碍司法公正。”

Poitras examines the suppression of this labor militancy in the special Luddite trials of 1812 and 1813. The twice-yearly Assizes, local sittings of courts for major crimes, proved to be too lenient for national authorities, so special trial commissions were set up in Lancaster, Chester, and York.  
波伊特拉斯考察了 1812 年和 1813 年卢德分子特别审判中对这种劳工激进主义的镇压。一年两次的巡回审判（地方法院审理重大犯罪案件）对国家当局来说过于宽容，因此在兰开斯特、切斯特和约克设立了特别审判委员会。

The records of these trials were well preserved, writes Poitras. At the Lancaster Special Assizes, held in May and June of 1812, four people were hung for setting fire to weaving mills with power looms; three were hung for Riot and Burglary; one (a woman) was hung for Riot, Larceny, and Grand Larceny; six others were transported for seven-year terms. At the Chester Special Commission trials of May 1812, five were sentenced to death; eight were transported for seven-year terms; eleven others had their death sentences commuted to transport for life. At the York Special Commission of January 1813, seventeen were sentenced to be hung; six were transported for seven-year terms; and one had his death sentence commuted to transport for life.  
波伊特拉斯写道，这些审判的记录保存完好。在1812年5月和6月举行的兰开斯特特别巡回审判中，四人因纵火焚烧使用动力织机的纺织厂而被处以绞刑；三人因暴乱和入室盗窃被处以绞刑；一人（一名女性）因暴乱、盗窃和重大盗窃被处以绞刑；另有六人被判处七年流放。在1812年5月举行的切斯特特别委员会审判中，五人被判处死刑；八人被判处七年流放；另有十一人的死刑被改判为终身流放。在1813年1月举行的约克特别委员会审判中，十七人被判处绞刑；六人被判处七年流放；一人的死刑被改判为终身流放。

At the beginning of the nineteenth century, there were more than 220 capital offenses on the English law books. Grand larceny, punishable by death, was for instance defined as the theft of goods worth more than 12 pence, about equivalent of £3.5 today. Many of these were for crimes against property; there has rarely been such an overt illustration of criminal justice as the servant of the class who writes the laws. (Up to the modest electoral reforms of 1832, a mere 4,500 men in Britain could vote in Parliamentary elections in a country of 2.6 million. The 1918 law that famously gave women the vote in the UK also did away with the last restrictions on male suffrage.)  
十九世纪初，英国法律条文中列有超过220种死刑罪名。例如，重大盗窃罪（可判处死刑）的定义是盗窃价值超过12便士（约合今天的3.5英镑）的物品。其中许多罪行都涉及财产犯罪；很少有比法律制定者为下属阶层服务更能体现刑事司法本质的例子了。（直到1832年那次温和的选举改革之前，在260万人口的英国，只有区区4500名男性拥有议会选举权。1918年那项赋予英国女性选举权的法律也废除了对男性选举权的最后限制。）

A confusing, overlapping series of statutes, including the Riot Act, Unlawful Oaths Act, Criminal Law Act of 1782, the Malicious Injury Act, and Malicious Shooting or Stabbing Act were added to by a burst of anti-Luddite panic law-making in 1812, Poitras writes. Five laws were introduced to deal specifically with the disturbances, including two Preservation of the Peace Acts and a Destruction of Stocking Frames, etc. Act, also known as the Frame-Breaking Act. The latter made the destruction of mechanized looms a capital felony. George Gordon Bryon made his debut speech in the House of Lords unsuccessfully arguing against this act, Poitras notes, defending the Luddites as “meager with famine, sullen with despair.”  
波伊特拉斯写道，一系列错综复杂、相互重叠的法令，包括《骚乱法案》、《非法宣誓法案》、《1782年刑法法案》、《恶意伤害法案》和《恶意枪击或刺伤法案》，在1812年因反卢德运动而引发的恐慌性立法浪潮中又被添加进来。五项专门针对骚乱的法律被提出，其中包括两项《维护和平法案》和一项《销毁织袜机等法案》（又称《破坏织机法案》）。后者将销毁机械织机定为死罪。波伊特拉斯指出，乔治·戈登·拜伦在英国上议院发表了他的首次演讲，反对这项法案，但未能成功。他辩称卢德分子“饥寒交迫，愁眉苦脸”。

This reign of legal terror would eventually be called the Bloody Code. It began to be undone with 1823’s Judgement of Death Act, which made the death penalty discretionary for most crimes. (The last execution in the UK took place in 1964.)  
这段法律恐怖统治最终被称为“血腥法典”。1823年的《死刑判决法案》开始瓦解这一法典，该法案使死刑对大多数罪行成为酌情判处。（英国最后一次执行死刑是在1964年。）

In practice, juries and even magistrates under the Bloody Code could show mercy against what many, not just the accused, thought extreme justice, Poitras explains. In grand larceny cases, for instance, juries were known to undervalue the amount of goods stolen—to save the accused from death or penal transportation. And, in fact, the earliest trials of the Luddites in the regular Assizes, were rather remarkably not bloody. For instance, at the Nottingham Lent Assize of March 1812, eighteen Luddites were brought to trial. No death penalties resulted; transportation for life was the harshest penalty meted out, and there were numerous acquittals.  
波伊特拉斯解释说，在实践中，根据《血腥法典》，陪审团甚至地方法官都可能对许多人（不仅仅是被告）认为过于严苛的判决表现出宽容。例如，在重大盗窃案中，陪审团有时会故意低估被盗物品的价值，以使被告免于死刑或流放。   事实上，卢德分子在常规巡回审判中的早期审判，出人意料地没有发生流血事件。例如，在 1812 年 3 月诺丁汉的四旬斋巡回审判中，18 名卢德分子受审。没有判处死刑；流放是当时判处的最严厉的刑罚，而且还有许多人被判无罪。

With its special commissions, the state would make sure that there would be much less mercy shown for the next round of Luddites brought to trial. Luddite crime was construed as a radical assault on property. Those fighting for their livelihoods would forfeit their lives. Luddism, as historian E. P. Thompson put it in his classic [_The Making of the English Working Class_](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/178147/the-making-of-the-english-working-class-by-e-p-thompson/), “ended on the scaffold.”  
通过设立特别委员会，国家确保下一批被送上法庭的卢德分子将得不到丝毫怜悯。卢德分子的罪行被视为对财产的激进攻击。那些为生计而战的人将付出生命的代价。正如历史学家 E·P·汤普森在其经典著作 [_《英国工人阶级的形成》_](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/178147/the-making-of-the-english-working-class-by-e-p-thompson/) 中所说，卢德主义“最终以绞刑架告终”。
